The problem

Big orders in thin tokens get slaughtered three ways at once.

01

The curve punishes you

Selling into a shallow pool moves the price against you with every token — you pay the impact even when a willing buyer exists somewhere.

02

The bots eat you

The moment your order is visible, sandwich bots trade around it. You created the move — they collect it.

03

The leak bleeds you

Even split into small clips, wallet-watchers spot the pattern by clip two and front-run the rest. The information is the cost.

How it works

Sealed orders. One batch. One price.

Trades happen in scheduled auctions instead of a public order book — so there is nothing to see and nothing to front-run.

Step 1 — Commit

Everyone submits sealed orders

Buyers and sellers commit hidden orders — side, size, and limit price encrypted, funds escrowed on-chain. No one can see the book, because there isn't one.

Step 2 — Reveal & match

The batch crosses at once

At auction time, orders are revealed, verified against their commitments, and matched together — size meets size directly, off the curve.

Step 3 — One fair price

Everyone clears at the same price

The whole batch settles at a single clearing price, bounded by an independent time-weighted market reference. No queue, no sequence, no one "in front of you" — sandwiching is impossible by construction.

Fair by design

Uniform price

Buyers and sellers in a batch all get the same price — there is no better position to steal.

Banded to reality

Batches only clear inside a band around a manipulation-resistant market reference.

No fishing

Committed orders post a bond — walk away from your reveal and you forfeit it.

Nothing leaks after

Unfilled interest is never disclosed. The market never learns what almost traded.
